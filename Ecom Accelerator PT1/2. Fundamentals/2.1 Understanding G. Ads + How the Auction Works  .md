# 2.1 Understanding G. Ads + How the Auction Works

*Converted from `PPC Co Pilot/Ecom Accelerator/2. Fundamentals/2.1 Understanding G. Ads + How the Auction Works  .rtfd`*

Google Ads is an online advertising platform that allows businesses to display their ads to potential customers on Google's search engine results pages (SERPs) and other Google properties.

When someone searches for a product or service related to your business, your ad can appear alongside the organic search results.

**How Google Ads Auction Works**

1. Introduction to the Google Ads Auction

- An automated process that determines which ads appear for a specific search and in what order
- Occurs every time a user performs a search on Google
- Designed to reward quality and relevance, not just highest bidder

4. Determining Ad Position

- User enters a search query
- Google identifies ads with keywords matching the search
- For each eligible ad, Google calculates the Ad Rank
- Ad Rank determines if and where an ad appears
- Ads are ordered based on Ad Rank
- Higher Ad Rank results in better ad positions

**Ad Rank**

Ad Rank is a value that Google uses to determine your ad position and whether your ads will show at all. It's calculated using several factors, but for simplicity, we'll focus on two main components:

1. Your bid amount
2. Your Quality Score

The basic formula for Ad Rank is:

Ad Rank = Max Bid × Quality Score

In our example, we have three advertisers:

1. Advertiser A: Bid $2.00, Quality Score 6 Ad Rank = $2.00 × 6 = 12
2. Advertiser B: Bid $4.00, Quality Score 3 Ad Rank = $4.00 × 3 = 12
3. Advertiser C: Bid $3.00, Quality Score 8 Ad Rank = $3.00 × 8 = 24

As you can see, despite Advertiser B having the highest bid at $4.00, they don't win the top position. Here's why:

1. Advertiser C wins the top position with the highest Ad Rank of 24. They achieved this with a moderate bid ($3.00) but an excellent Quality Score (8).
2. Advertiser A takes the second position. Although they have the same Ad Rank as Advertiser B (12), Google typically favors the ad with the higher Quality Score when Ad Ranks are equal.
3. Advertiser B, despite having the highest bid, ends up in the third position due to their low Quality Score.

This example illustrates a key principle of Google Ads: a high-quality ad with a lower bid can outperform a low-quality ad with a higher bid. This system encourages advertisers to focus not just on bidding high, but also on creating relevant, high-quality ads and landing pages.

**Quality Score**

![1d63653f9caf4bb682958c0331e44bc071f5a84eb36e42409146a0f429587ad1.png](2.1%20Understanding%20G.%20Ads%20%2B%20How%20the%20Auction%20Works%20%20.assets/1d63653f9caf4bb682958c0331e44bc071f5a84eb36e42409146a0f429587ad1.png)

1. Expected Click-Through Rate (CTR): This is a prediction of how likely your ad is to be clicked when shown for a specific keyword. It's based on the keyword's past performance and the historical CTR of the ads in the same position. A higher CTR indicates that users find your ad helpful and relevant.
2. Ad Relevance: This measures how closely your ad matches the intent behind a user's search. It looks at how well your keywords align with your ad text and how relevant they are to what the user is searching for.
3. Landing Page Experience: This evaluates the quality and relevance of your landing page to users who click on your ad. Google considers factors such as:

- Relevance of content to the ad and keyword
- Transparency and trustworthiness
- Ease of navigation
- Page load time
- Mobile-friendliness

Google combines these three factors to determine your Quality Score, which is represented on a scale from 1 to 10, with 10 being the highest. A higher Quality Score can lead to:

- Lower costs per click
- Better ad positions
- Higher chances of your ads being shown

It's important to note that Quality Score is calculated every time your keyword matches a search query, so it can fluctuate based on various factors including user location, device, time of day, and other auction-time signals.

To improve your Quality Score:

1. Improve your CTR:

- Write compelling ad copy that matches user intent
- Use ad extensions to provide more information
- Organize keywords into tightly themed ad groups

2. Enhance Ad Relevance:

- Ensure your keywords are closely related to your ad text
- Use the keyword in your ad headline and description when appropriate
- Create specific ads for each ad group

3. Optimize Landing Page Experience:

- Ensure your landing page content is relevant to your ad and keywords
- Improve page load speed
- Make your site mobile-friendly
- Provide clear and useful information
- Make navigation easy and intuitive

**Actual CPC Calculation in Google Ads**

![688d3eff5f714397bf8548f8a59af5548f0e17510f4c4f1db5e84ae4ec20681a.png](2.1%20Understanding%20G.%20Ads%20%2B%20How%20the%20Auction%20Works%20%20.assets/688d3eff5f714397bf8548f8a59af5548f0e17510f4c4f1db5e84ae4ec20681a.png)

1. Ad Positions: Based on the Ad Ranks, the positions are:

Note: When Ad Ranks are tied, the advertiser with the higher Quality Score gets the better position. - Advertiser C: 1st (Ad Rank 24) - Advertiser A: 2nd (Ad Rank 12) - Advertiser B: 3rd (Ad Rank 12) 2. Actual CPC Calculation for C (1st position): Actual CPC = (Ad Rank of the advertiser below / Quality Score of C) + $0.01 = (12 / 8) + $0.01 = $1.51 So, even though Advertiser C bid $3.00, they only pay $1.51 per click. 3. Actual CPC Calculation for A (2nd position): Actual CPC = (Ad Rank of the advertiser below / Quality Score of A) + $0.01 = (12 / 6) + $0.01 = $2.01 In this case, Advertiser A ends up paying $0.01 more than their max bid. This can happen when the Ad Rank of the advertiser below is equal to or higher than your own.

This example illustrates several important points:

1. Higher Quality Score can lead to better ad position even with a lower bid: Advertiser C won the top position with a $3.00 bid, beating Advertiser B's $4.00 bid, thanks to a much higher Quality Score.
2. You can pay significantly less than your maximum bid: Advertiser C is paying only $1.51, despite bidding $3.00.
3. In some cases, you might pay your full bid (or slightly more): Advertiser A is paying $2.01, which is $0.01 more than their $2.00 bid. This happens because the advertiser below them (B) has the same Ad Rank.
4. Tied Ad Ranks are broken by Quality Score: Although A and B have the same Ad Rank, A gets the higher position due to better Quality Score.

**Factors To Consider**

1. Factors Influencing Auction Dynamics

- Time of day and seasonality
- Geographic location of the searcher
- Device type (mobile, desktop, tablet)
- Changes in competitor strategies

2. Strategies for Success in the Google Ads Auction

- Focus on improving Quality Score through relevant ads and landing pages
- Regularly review and adjust bids
- Utilize ad assets to enhance ad relevance and appeal
- Monitor performance metrics and competitor activities

3. Common Misconceptions

- Highest bid doesn't always win
- Auction results can change even if you don't modify your campaign

4. Advanced Auction Considerations

- Impact of ad formats (e.g., shopping ads, video ads) on the auction
