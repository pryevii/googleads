**Optimization Fundamentals**

At the end of the day you are always trying to do the following:

More good, less bad.

That is the forever game you are playing.

It is up to you to define what is good - e.g. 300% ROAS target for eCommerce or maybe a $50 CPA for lead form fill for led gen

**Search campaigns are composed of the following:**

- Campaign (settings)
- Ad Group
- Keywords
- Ads

To start optimizing we work top down.

**Campaign Structure**

The key to structure is to consolidate as much as possible but only when it makes sense.

**When can it make sense to segment campaigns?**

- Brand vs Non-brand (never mix these two)
- Location (international targeting especially since you have different timezones and audiences)
- Categories (e.g. TVs vs Video Games)
- Different goals

**What no longer works:**

- SKAGs (single keyword ad groups)
- Segmenting campaigns by Alpha Beta method (my old favorite 🙂)
- Segmenting by match types
- Segmenting by device

**Campaign Settings**

Having the incorrect campaign settings can make or break campaign performance.

**Settings to check:**

- Conversion tracking is set correctly in Goal setting
- Network settings do not include Display (you can test Search partners - if it works, it work if it doesn’t, it doesn’t)
- Location targeting is correct and Location target is set to ‘Presence’
  ![Attachment.png](How%20to%20Optimize%20Search%20Campaigns.assets/Attachment.png)
- Budget (are top performing campaigns limited by budget?)
- Ad schedule (does ad schedule make sense?)
- Devices (check performance)
- Audiences (check performance)

**Ad Group Structure**

Like campaigns, your goal of ad groups is to consolidate as much as you can and segment only when it makes sense.

The goal of your ad group is to group keywords and ads that match together and will resonate with the searcher. (Hence the name Ad Group)

The simplest structure is usually an ad group per unique landing page you want to target or using the STAG method - which is Single Themed Ad Group.

**When to segment or create new ad groups?**

- You can create a better ad or landing page for a set of keywords
- You have an ad group or set of keywords that need more attention and need to move to own campaign (e.g. not getting any impressions or clicks)
- Ad group or keyword performance is significantly different and you want to isolate out poor performers

**Keywords**

**Keyword optimization comes down to the following:**

- Pause poor performing keywords
- Add new keywords from search query report
- Add new negative keywords

**When to pause poor performing keywords?**
There is no hard rule here but you’ll know a loser when you see a loser. If a keyword has had 100s of clicks and is showing significantly lower conv. rate and is significantly below goal then you are probably better off pausing.

**When to add new keywords?**

If a search term has at least 30 impressions per day month (1 per day) and has had shown signs of good performance then I like to add and test these keywords.

**What match type to add new keywords in?**

If you are using all broad match keywords successfully then add new keywords in all broad match.

I also like adding top performing keywords in exact match form to see my impression share metrics - this doesn’t always mean exact match of the keyword will perform well but I like to give them a test.

If you are not using broad match keywords fully and you are using phrase and exact then continue to add keywords in exact match or phrase match.

**What to do with keywords you add?**

- Add them to existing ad group
- Create a new ad group for these keywords (if you can create more relevant ad copy)

<aside>
💡 Don’t add keywords just to add keywords. It just creates an absolute mess and is unnecessary in the day of age of machine learning.

</aside>

**How often do you add negative keywords?**

Adding negative keywords should be done frequently. For smaller accounts that might be biweekly or once per month. For large volume accounts that could be daily.

**Bidding**

Bidding is technically a Campaign setting. However, I’ve included it towards the end as a separate section since bidding will not save an account with poor campaign structure, incorrect settings, and bad keywords and ad groups.

A lot of people bank too much of their success on bidding. Bidding just steers the math of the campaign/ad groups to hit your goal.

Your success is going to come from everything else discussed. Bidding is just the steering wheel.

With that said, you should be testing and adjusting bids when necessary. Bidding is a large topic so I’ve included an entire masterclass on Bidding that I recorded on YouTube:

**Auction Insights Report**

Auction Insights report gives you data on competitors who you are competing with in the same ad auctions for your keywords.

You should be reviewing the Auction Insights report frequently to make sure you don’t see any changes in competitor aggressiveness or spot any new competitors entering the ad auctions.
