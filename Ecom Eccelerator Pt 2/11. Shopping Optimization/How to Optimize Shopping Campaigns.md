**Campaign Structure**

Campaign structure is one of the most important keys to allowing for your ads to get maximized performance.

The goal is to maximize best sellers.

The structure depends on size of account and skus:

- For lots of skus and brands, group by Tiers for your middle of the pack Brands and Product Categories.
- For smaller spenders, keep this simple. Focus on Top Sellers and group your other products into a catch all campaign.

**When can it make sense to segment campaigns?**

- Best sellers
- Product categories or Brands
- On Sale Products
- New Products
- Seasonal Products
- Zombies (0 click products)
- Query Sculpting

**Query Sculpting**

Query Sculpting was introduced by [Martin Roettgerding](https://www.ppc-epiphany.com/2014/12/08/taking-google-shopping-to-the-next-level/) that structures the account into three campaigns:

1. Product Campaign
2. Brand Campaign
3. Others Campaign

Negative keywords and campaign priority settings are used to filter search queries into the appropriate campaign.

Brand names are added as negative keywords in the highest priority campaign to ensure brand searches show listings from the Brand Campaign.

The advantages are that you can analyze query performance separately and set bids accordingly for each campaign type.

**Bidding & Budgets**

Budgets should be allocated in a manner where best sellers are not being limited.

When you are query sculpting make sure you are using a shared budget between your campaign splits.

For bids, you ideally want to be running tROAS once your campaign(s) is dialed in. However, in the beginning of a new account or new campaign you’ll usually need to work on Manual CPC or you can test tROAS at a very small ROAS target (e.g. <100%)

**Exclude Poor Performing Products**

Exclude poor performing products from your campaigns and product ad groups.

Analyze why they are poor performing:

**Key questions to ask:**

- Are there any product errors?
- Is the title optimized?
- Is the price competitive?
- Is this just a seasonal dip?

If I have budget to spare then I will test running a “Loser” product campaign with very small bids and isolate out products that I was going to exclude as one last chance to see if I can make them work.

**Performance Monitoring**

**Performance Metrics**

When reviewing performance of your Shopping campaigns and products your usual metrics are still going to be used (Clicks, CTR, Avg. CPC, Conv. rate, Conv Value, Conv. Value / Cost)

However, with Shopping there are some unique metrics you can review that will give you further insights.

**Key metrics to review:**

- Search Impression Share
- Click Share
- Benchmark CPC (Cost per Click)
- Benchmark CTR (Click Through Rate)

**Auction Insights Report**

Auction Insights report gives you data on competitors who you are competing with in the same ad auctions for your keywords.

You should be reviewing the Auction Insights report frequently to make sure you don’t see any changes in competitor aggressiveness or spot any new competitors entering the ad auctions.
