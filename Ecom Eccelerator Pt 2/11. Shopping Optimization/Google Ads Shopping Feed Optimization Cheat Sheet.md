# Google Ads Shopping Feed Optimization Cheat Sheet

*Converted from `PPC Co Pilot/Ecom Accelerator/11. Shopping Optimization/Google Ads Shopping Feed Optimization Cheat Sheet.rtfd`*

**Required feed attributes**

- id (Unique identifier for each product)
- title (Product title)
- description (Product description)
- price (Product price)
- availability (Product availability)
- link (Product URL)
- image\_link (Product image URL)
- brand (Product brand)
- gtin (Global Trade Item Number)

**Optional feed attributes**

- product\_type (Product type)
- mpn (Manufacturer Part Number) **[Recommended]**
- google\_product\_category (Google product category) **[Recommended]**
- availability (Product availability)
- color (Product color)
- size (Product size)
- weight (Product weight)
- shipping (Product shipping)
- condition (Product condition)
- age\_group (Product age group)
- gender (Product gender)
- material (Product material)
- pattern (Product pattern)
- occasion (Product occasion)
- scent (Product scent)
- features (Product features)
- custom\_label (0-4) (Custom attributes) **[Recommended]**

**Biggest Needle Movers**

- Price 🔥🔥🔥🔥🔥

- Make sure to check Merchant Center often and review your Diagnostics tab for any feed or product issues.

**Price**

- Price has shown to always win. If you sell the same product as a competitor the lowest price will win no matter how the rest of your feed is. It is proven to increase impression share and clicks.
- Don’t try sneaky price baiting tricks - Google will catch this eventually

**Title**

- Title optimization can really increase impressions and clicks. This is the most important attribute of your feed that you can control.
- Word order matters - keep the most important keywords in front of title. Google only reads 150 Characters and titles shown in the SERP can cut off at 30 characters.
- Consider how people are searching for your products? Do people search for the Brand first? Do they search model or part numbers? Do they search by product type or category?
- Check your Search Query Reports and see what your highest converting keywords are for products that you can bake into your product titles.

**Title Structure Ideas**

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Clothing/Apparel** | **Brand + Gender + Product Type + Color + Size + Material** |
| Consumables | Brand + Product Type + Attribution (weight/count) |
| Hard Goods | Brand + Product + Attributes (size, weight, quantity) |
| Electronics | Brand + Attributes + Product Type + Model # |
| Books | Title + Type + Format + Author |
| Seasonal | Occasion + Product Type + Attributes |

- You’ll notice most title structures put Brand in front of Title. If your Brand is unknown or not important in how buyers search then push Brand to the back of the Title and start with the most important attribute or keyword.

**Example:** Kid & Pet Friendly Sectional Sofa | Washable | Home Reserve

**Title Optimization Case Study:**

Simply changing the Title from a generic title to an optimized title for this online furniture company lead to a 10x in clicks and impressions

**Old Title:** Ali Sofa in Oscar Sterling
**New Title:** Kid & Pet Friendly Sectional Sofa | Washable | Ali in Oscar Sterling | Home Reserve

**GTIN**

- Google has put a greater emphasis on GTIN over the last few years. You can find your GTINs from your supplier, manufacture, or barcode lookup.

You can search for GTINs here: <https://gepir.gs1.org/index.php/search-by-gtin>

**Image**

- Just make sure you have high quality images that are transparent or white background with no watermarks or text.
- If you are taking photos of your products on your iPhone on your desk - your images suck.
- Test different images for your top sellers to stand out from your competition (e.g. Lifestyle photos vs Studio)

**Description**

- It’s the same idea as product titles here: You want to include direct keywords in your product descriptions whenever possible. You can use the primary keyword again, and you can also use alternative secondary keywords if you want to really cover your bases.
- Try and bake in core keywords in the product description, as you can see here:

![Attachment.png](Google%20Ads%20Shopping%20Feed%20Optimization%20Cheat%20Sheet.assets/Attachment.png)

- We’ve tested description optimizations but it doesn’t move the needle like Title optimizations. Don’t ignore descriptions but don’t focus too much on them either.

**Category**

- Although category is an optional attribute, having the correct Google Product Categories will increase your chances of showing for the correct search terms.
- You can review Google’s Product Taxonomy here: <https://www.google.com/basepages/producttype/taxonomy.en-US.txt>
- If you don’t include a product category Google will auto-apply what it believes is the best category.

**Custom Labels**

- Custom labels won’t actually impact your feed quality at all. However, use of Custom labels can significantly help with account structure and data analysis optimizations which in many cases can be a game changer for Shopping ad performance.
- Custom label ideas:

- Bestsellers
- Margin
- Seasonality
- Price Tier (e.g. $0-$100)
- New products

- Don't overload your feed with too many custom labels. This can make it difficult to manage and track your data.

**Other Attributes**

Add as many relevant product attributes to your product feed as possible. It tells Google what your product is and exactly what characteristics it has. That goes a long way in helping them show your listing in the right place and for the right searches.

Google, after all, can’t take a look at a picture and quite tell the difference between a pair of black and white running shoes and a penguin laying flat. (At least not yet). It’s up to us to explain what the products are.

And remember that the more attributes you give to your products, the more opportunities you have to show up when users apply filters to their Shopping results.

![word-image-11.png](Google%20Ads%20Shopping%20Feed%20Optimization%20Cheat%20Sheet.assets/word-image-11.png)

There are so many attributes that you can choose from, and while you don’t need to add all or even most to the product title, you want to add them all here (even when not required). Pay special attention to attributes that may shift buying choices.

And keep in mind that you can use different attributes for campaign segmentation when you’re monitoring or managing Shopping Ad campaigns. This allows you to take actions like running ads only for specific product types, or to give unique bids to certain product categories.
